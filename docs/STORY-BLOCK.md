## WHAT
*(Definition, purpose, function)*

The sovereign musician's instrument. Capture, layer, and shape music that does
not exist yet — on your own device, with nothing leaving it.

**It is the creator's half of what used to be one app.** Its sibling,
**resonance-compass**, is a media player for music you already hold the rights
to: find it, understand it, cut it, mix it. This repo is for the sound you make.

## WHY
*(Need, purpose, problem solved)*

**Because the friction was diagnostic, and we kept treating it as bugs.**

Through one long night and the morning after, the same complaints kept arriving
in different clothes: the comfort bar at the foot of the screen played the
*library's* last song instead of the studio mix; recording produced **two play
buttons, from two different tools**; the transport wanted to belong to the room
you were standing in and couldn't.

Each of those had a plausible local fix. None of them was a bug.

**A listener's instrument and a creator's instrument want opposite things from
the same transport**, and no amount of careful reconciliation makes one shell
serve both honestly. KP saw it and said so:

> *"we need to separate the resonance compass and musicians compass to make
> this right. maybe the musicians compass belongs in resonance-sistrum, the
> compass remains a media player of licensed materials the user holds rights
> to."*

That sentence dissolved a whole category of open problems rather than solving
them one at a time. **This repo exists because the architecture was already
telling us, and we were reading it as defects.**

## INSPIRATION
*(Origin story, seed moment, what sparked it)*

The seed is older than the split: **v3, commissioned 2026-07-18**, for a
musician the market never served — capture the melody before it escapes, layer
takes like the old four-tracks, plan a set by the energy of the room.

And the naming question left open that day — *"Musician's Compass" or
"Resonance Studio"?* — **stood unanswered for twenty-five days because both
answers were wrong.** It was never a mode of the Compass and never a studio
bolted to it. It was a different instrument, and the question could only be
answered by giving it its own house.

## ETYMOLOGY
*(If discovered, not invented — origin event, recognition event,
temporal span, thread, emotional valence)*

**Sistrum** — Latin, from Greek *seistron*, "that which is shaken," from *seiō*,
"I shake."

KP's own definition, given verbatim when he named it:

> *"A sacred percussive instrument historically shaken in the worship of the
> Egyptian goddess Isis or Hathor. Its rhythmic rattling was believed to
> manipulate energy and ward off negative spirits."*

**The name was not decoration laid over the app — it described what was already
built.** The *manipulate energy* half was already in v3's own plan: the Setlist
Builder organizes *"by energy, key, tempo, emotional arc,"* on the mood
folksonomy this lineage has kept from the start. The *ward* half is the sensory
law the whole family is built under — no urgency, no shame, gentle empty states,
a metronome that is time you can *see*, `prefers-reduced-motion` honored.

**An instrument that wards is not a poetic claim here. It is the acceptance
criteria.**

And the pairing holds the split in the names themselves: **a compass orients
you; a sistrum is held in the hand and played.** One finds the way. One makes
the sound.
